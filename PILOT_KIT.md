# GharSe — Pilot Ops Kit

Everything needed to run the first cell **before** the app is doing the heavy lifting.
One neighbourhood, ~25 kitchens, ~200 customers. Print this, work through it.

Figures marked ⚠️ change from time to time — verify on the official source before quoting them
to anyone.

---

## 0. Four weeks, in order

| Week | Goal | Done when |
|------|------|-----------|
| 1 | **Demand proof.** Talk to PG residents. No app, no cooks, no promises. | 20 people say they'd pay ~₹100/day for lunch, 20+ days a month, and 5 give you an advance for week one |
| 2 | **Supply.** Find and verify 3–5 cooks within 2 km of those PGs. | 3 kitchens with FSSAI applied for, UPI IDs on file, a tasting done |
| 3 | **Run it manually.** WhatsApp group + UPI + one delivery run at 12:45. | 5 consecutive days served, no missed deliveries |
| 4 | **Move it into the app.** Onboard the same people onto GharSe. | Orders, payments and payouts all flowing through the app |

Do not skip to week 4. The app is a way to scale something that already works, not a way to find
out whether it works.

---

## 1. Demand: the PG conversation

Ask, don't pitch. Five questions, two minutes:

1. What did you eat for lunch yesterday, and what did it cost?
2. How many days a week do you eat outside food you didn't enjoy?
3. If a home kitchen 1 km away sent a fresh veg/non-veg meal to your reception at 1 PM for ₹100,
   how many days a month would you take it?
4. Would you pay for a month up front if it were ₹90 a meal instead?
5. Any allergies, or food you can't eat?

**Record every answer in a sheet.** Name, PG, phone, meals/month, veg or non-veg, budget.
That sheet is your launch list; it is worth more than the code.

**The bar:** 20 people saying 18+ meals a month. If the honest answer is 6–8 meals a month, this
neighbourhood isn't the one — go to the next PG cluster, don't lower the bar.

**Pitch, once they've answered:**

> I'm starting a thing where women cooking at home nearby send fresh lunch to your PG reception
> every day. ₹100 a meal, ₹2,600 for the month, pause any day you're travelling. First week is
> ₹50 a meal so you can taste it before committing. Want in?

## 2. Supply: finding the cooks

Where they actually are: apartment WhatsApp groups, temple and church noticeboards, tailoring
shops, anganwadi and school-gate conversations at 3 PM, and — most reliably — referrals from the
first cook you sign. Ask every cook: *"Who else in this area cooks better than you?"*

**Screening call, five minutes:**

- How many meals can you cook in one go, comfortably, without help?
- Which cuisine do you cook best? (Take her at her word — specialists beat generalists.)
- Do you have a separate gas point and clean water?
- Can you have food packed and ready by 12:15 every weekday?
- Do you have a UPI ID in your own name? *(If not, that's the first thing to fix — her earnings
  should land in her account, not her husband's.)*
- Would you rather cook 20 meals every single day, or 40 once a week?

**Then taste the food before promising anything.** Pay for that meal.

### Cook onboarding checklist

- [ ] Tasting done and approved
- [ ] Kitchen seen in person — separate cooking area, clean water, covered storage, no pets in the cooking space
- [ ] Aadhaar / ID photographed, phone number verified with a test call
- [ ] **UPI ID in her own name**, tested with a ₹1 transfer
- [ ] FSSAI registration applied for (§3)
- [ ] Hygiene basics covered (§4)
- [ ] Photos taken: 3 of the food, 1 of her kitchen, 1 of her (only if she's comfortable)
- [ ] Capacity agreed in writing: how many meals, which slot, which days
- [ ] Price agreed, and the ₹-split shown to her on paper (§5)
- [ ] Added to GharSe, verified in admin, FSSAI number entered, first listing published
- [ ] Added to the ops WhatsApp group

## 3. FSSAI, in plain language

A home food business is a Food Business Operator and needs FSSAI registration. Two tiers matter:

| Turnover | What she needs | ⚠️ Fee |
|----------|----------------|--------|
| Up to ₹12 lakh/year | **Basic Registration** (Form A) | ~₹100 per year |
| ₹12 lakh – ₹20 crore | **State Licence** (Form B) | ~₹2,000–₹5,000 per year |

Every cook in the pilot will be in the first row.

**How to do it with her** — sit next to her, don't send a link:

1. Go to **foscos.fssai.gov.in** → *Apply for Licence/Registration*
2. State: Karnataka → Business type: **Petty Food Business Operator / Home-based**
3. Fill Form A with her details and the kitchen address
4. Upload: passport photo, photo ID (Aadhaar/voter/PAN), proof of the kitchen address, and a
   simple declaration of the food she'll make
5. Pay the fee, note the **application reference number**
6. The registration certificate usually arrives within a few weeks; the reference number is
   enough to get started on the app once you've verified her kitchen yourself

**Rules that follow from it:** the certificate must be displayed in the kitchen, and the FSSAI
number should appear on food packaging. Print her number on the meal label stickers — it looks
professional and it's the law's intent.

**Do not let a kitchen sell food on the platform before this is at least applied for.** One food
safety incident ends the company; the FSSAI gate is not paperwork, it's the insurance policy.

## 4. Hygiene: the 10 things to actually say

Cover this in 20 minutes, in her kitchen, in her language:

1. Wash hands with soap before cooking and after handling raw meat
2. Separate boards and knives for raw meat and everything else
3. Cook chicken and mutton through — no pink at the bone
4. Never keep cooked food at room temperature more than 2 hours
5. Pack hot food hot; don't seal it steaming, or it sweats and spoils
6. Hair tied and covered while cooking
7. Fresh oil — no reusing yesterday's frying oil
8. Wash vegetables before cutting, not after
9. If she or anyone at home has fever, loose motions or a skin infection: no cooking that day.
   **She still gets paid for the orders we move elsewhere** — otherwise she'll hide it
10. Label every container: dish, date, veg/non-veg, her FSSAI number

Print these ten as a sheet and stick it in every kitchen.

## 5. Pricing: the sheet to fill with her

Work it out *with* the cook, on paper, so she owns the number.

**Cost per meal** — rice/roti + dal + vegetable + protein + oil/masala + gas + packaging.
Packaging alone is ₹8–12 a meal; nobody remembers it, and it's the difference between profit and
break-even.

**Worked example — a ₹85 veg meal:**

| Line | ₹ |
|------|---|
| Ingredients | 38 |
| Gas + water + electricity | 4 |
| Packaging (box, lid, bag, sticker) | 10 |
| **Her cost** | **52** |
| Her price on the app | 85 |
| Platform commission (8%) | −6.80 |
| **She receives** | **78.20** |
| **Her margin per meal** | **₹26.20** |

At 20 meals a day, 25 days: **≈ ₹13,100 a month** from one slot. Show her this table, not a
promise. Then check the real numbers after week one and correct them together.

**Rules of thumb:** price at roughly 1.6–1.8× her true cost. Never let her price below cost to
"get started" — a cook who loses money quits in three weeks, and she'll be right to.

**Set the customer price against the local reality:** find the two nearest ₹100–150 lunch options
and enter that as the "typical outside price" on the listing, so the saving shown in the app is
real.

## 6. Day-one operating rhythm

Before the app carries it, run the day on WhatsApp and UPI.

**Two groups, that's all:**
- *GharSe Kitchens* — you + the cooks
- *GharSe HSR Lunch* — you + the customers

| Time | What happens |
|------|--------------|
| 8:00 PM (day before) | Post tomorrow's menu + price in the customer group |
| 9:30 PM | Cut-off. Count the orders. Post the count to each cook |
| 10:00 PM | Cooks confirm; you send each customer their UPI amount |
| 11:00 AM | Cooks pack; you collect UTRs and note who hasn't paid |
| 12:15 PM | Pickup run — kitchens in one loop |
| 12:45 PM | Drop at PG receptions; one photo of the drop into the customer group |
| 2:00 PM | Ask three customers what they thought. Every day, three different people |
| 6:00 PM | Pay the cooks for yesterday. Never later than this |

**Non-negotiables:** the cook is paid within 24 hours of delivery, whatever happened with the
customer's payment. Absorb that gap yourself in the pilot — trust is bought once and it's cheap
at this price.

## 7. When it goes wrong

| Situation | What you do |
|-----------|-------------|
| Cook falls ill at 10 AM | Move her orders to another kitchen; she is still paid for what was ordered |
| Food arrives cold | Refund that meal, no argument, same day. Then fix the packing or the route |
| Customer complains of illness | Stop that kitchen's listings immediately, keep a sample if there is one, call the customer twice — day 1 and day 3. Never argue |
| Customer doesn't pay | One reminder, then stop the subscription. Don't let it run to ₹1,000 |
| Cook wants to sell to the customer directly | Expect it. Your answer is the demand you bring, the payments you handle and the days she doesn't have to sell. If she still leaves, wish her well — you're not a cage |
| Rider doesn't turn up | You deliver. Every founder in this business has done the 12:45 run themselves |

## 8. What to measure, weekly

Five numbers on one line in a sheet, every Sunday:

1. **Meals per customer this month** — the one that decides everything. Target 18+
2. Active customers who ordered ≥1 meal this week
3. Kitchen utilisation — meals sold ÷ capacity offered
4. On-time rate (delivered before 1:15 PM)
5. Rupees paid to cooks this week

If (1) is climbing, keep going and don't add features. If it's flat after three weeks, the
problem is the food or the timing — go eat the meals yourself for a week and you'll know which.

---

## 9. Moving it into the app

Once week 3 works, in this order:

1. Admin → **Fees & Settings** → platform UPI ID, commission, delivery fees
2. Admin → verify each cook, enter her FSSAI number
3. Cook → profile, photo, UPI ID, then her listings with photos
4. Cook → a meal plan (26 days) so subscribers stop ordering daily
5. Customers register and subscribe; keep the WhatsApp group running in parallel for a fortnight
6. Admin → **WhatsApp Outbox** each evening: one tap per message, no Meta account needed
7. Admin → **Payouts** every evening at 6 PM

The app's job is to remove the spreadsheet and the mental arithmetic. It is not the business.
The business is 25 women who can now sell what they already know how to make.
